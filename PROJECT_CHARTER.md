# KreyolBench Project Charter

KreyolBench is a Kreyol-first Haitian Creole AI research project focused on public-interest NLP, speech, evaluation, and language technology infrastructure. The project exists to support rigorous research, open tools, ethical datasets, and practical systems for education, public health, civic information, disaster response, digital preservation, and language equity.

This charter defines the project mission, boundaries, values, and decision model. It is operational guidance, not legal advice. Formal contracts, IRB/ethics review, restricted data sharing, funding agreements, and disputed authorship should be reviewed with qualified institutional or legal counsel.

## Mission

KreyolBench aims to make Haitian Creole more visible and better supported in the global AI ecosystem by producing:

- Reproducible benchmarks and evaluation protocols.
- Open-source code and research infrastructure.
- Ethically governed datasets and annotation workflows.
- Baseline models and error analyses.
- Publications, grant proposals, and community-facing tools.
- Statistical methods for reliable low-resource AI evaluation.

## Project Lead

The founding project lead retains final responsibility for project direction, public representation, release approval, access control, and dispute escalation unless ownership is formally transferred to an institution or steering committee.

Maintainers and working-group leads may make scoped decisions within their assigned areas, but decisions involving public release, restricted data, authorship, funding, major partnerships, or project mission require project-lead approval.

## Values

- **Public benefit:** prioritize education, civic access, public health, disaster response, digital inclusion, and cultural preservation.
- **Scientific rigor:** publish claims only when supported by documented data, methods, evaluation, and limitations.
- **Language respect:** treat Haitian Creole as a language of serious scientific, cultural, civic, and educational importance.
- **Community credit:** acknowledge Haitian Creole speakers, annotators, educators, linguists, and community contributors.
- **Ethical data practice:** collect only what is needed, document sources, review licenses, and protect sensitive data.
- **Reproducibility:** make code, schemas, configs, evaluation scripts, and non-sensitive artifacts reusable.
- **Least privilege:** grant collaborators only the access needed for their current role.

## In Scope

- Haitian Creole NLP benchmarks.
- Haitian Creole ASR, TTS, MT, retrieval, normalization, and LLM evaluation.
- Annotation guidelines, datasets, data cards, model cards, and leaderboards.
- Statistical reliability for low-resource AI evaluation.
- Public-interest prototypes for education, health, civic access, and disaster response.
- Publications, workshops, grants, and open-source releases aligned with the mission.

## Out of Scope

KreyolBench must not be used to support:

- Surveillance, repression, or profiling of Haitian Creole speakers.
- Automated denial of public services, aid, education, or legal access.
- Harmful targeting based on language, dialect, geography, politics, or migration status.
- Unconsented release of human-subject, speech, or private community data.
- Claims of complete dialect, regional, or population coverage without evidence.
- Redistribution of third-party data without permission.

## Asset Classification

- **Public assets:** README, docs, code, schemas, synthetic/sample data, public issues, public releases.
- **Private assets:** unpublished paper drafts, grant drafts, reviewer notes, roadmaps, partner lists, internal meeting notes.
- **Sensitive assets:** raw speech, consent records, annotator identity, private communications, PII-risk data, restricted corpora, non-redistributable sources.

Sensitive assets require explicit approval and must not be stored in public Git history.

## Decision Model

Routine decisions are made by maintainers through pull-request review and documented issue discussion.

Project-lead approval is required for:

- Public dataset or model release.
- Changes to licensing or data policy.
- Access to restricted or sensitive data.
- Submission of papers using project data or branding.
- Grant submissions in the project name.
- New institutional partnerships.
- Authorship dispute resolution.
- Security or data-leak response.

## Review Cycle

This charter should be reviewed at least every six months or after any major incident, funding award, public release, or governance dispute.
