# Authorship and Credit Policy

KreyolBench uses transparent contribution tracking and fair credit practices. Authorship is not automatic for joining a chat, attending meetings, or making a small one-time contribution.

This policy is operational guidance. Formal academic disputes should follow the relevant journal, conference, university, or funder rules.

## Authorship Principles

Authorship may be appropriate when a contributor makes substantial intellectual or technical contributions such as:

- Research design.
- Dataset design or major data creation.
- Annotation framework design or expert linguistic review.
- Model development or substantial experiments.
- Statistical analysis or evaluation methodology.
- Major writing, editing, or response to reviewers.
- Release engineering that materially supports the scientific contribution.

Authors should also approve the final manuscript and take responsibility for their contribution.

## Acknowledgement

Acknowledgement may be appropriate for:

- Small documentation fixes.
- One-off feedback.
- Limited annotation.
- Community review.
- Administrative help.
- Small bug reports.
- Short literature suggestions.

## Contribution Tracking

Track contributions using:

- Git commits.
- Pull requests.
- Issue assignments.
- Annotation logs.
- Meeting notes.
- Paper section ownership.
- Release checklists.
- CRediT taxonomy where appropriate.

Recommended CRediT categories include conceptualization, data curation, formal analysis, funding acquisition, investigation, methodology, project administration, resources, software, supervision, validation, visualization, writing original draft, and writing review and editing.

## Authorship Order

Authorship order should reflect:

- Intellectual leadership.
- Size and importance of contribution.
- Responsibility for writing and submission.
- Field norms for the target venue.
- Any prior written agreement.

Discuss authorship order before submission, not after acceptance.

## Paper Draft Access

Paper drafts should use access levels:

- Public outline.
- Core writing-team draft.
- Advisor/reviewer draft.
- Preprint or public release.

Do not broadly share full drafts before authorship, data rights, and submission strategy are clear.

## Disputes

Authorship disputes should be raised early. The project lead may appoint a small authorship review group or ask an external advisor to review the contribution record.
